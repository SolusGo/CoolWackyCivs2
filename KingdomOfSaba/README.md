# The Kingdom of Saba

**Karib'il Watar** leads a modest historical civilization of ancient South Arabian water management. Capital: **Ma'rib**. Preferred victories: **Science / Culture**. Complexity: **2/5**. Requires Civilization V: Brave New World and Windows Community Patch v151; Community Balance Patch/Vox Populi is not required. Human-playable only (`Playable=1`, `AIPlayable=0`); no AI strategy is provided.

| Unique | Exact effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Where the Desert Blooms** | Each River city receives +1 Food/+1 Production. Each city centered on Desert, including Desert Hills, receives +1 Culture. Conditions stack and apply while Saba owns the city, including acquired cities. |
| **Wadi Sentinel — Spearman** | All active CP Spearman properties, +15% strength when defending on underlying Desert and +1 Sight. Both unique bonuses are lost on upgrade. Ordinary inherited promotions follow native rules. |
| **Ma'rib Cistern — Water Mill** | All active CP Water Mill properties, requirements and effects, plus +1 Food/+1 Gold. Uses the same building class; Saba cannot separately construct the ordinary Water Mill. |

The ability adds flat city yields. Neighboring Desert, nearby water graphics and Oasis fresh water do not qualify the city center. A Desert River city with a Cistern receives +2 Food/+1 Production/+1 Culture/+1 Gold beyond a normal CP Water Mill. Cistern requirements remain those of the active Water Mill.

Settle suitable River sites, use Desert city centers where growth remains viable, and build Cisterns to support population and the treasury. Wadi Sentinels defend dry approaches and reveal threats; their promotion adds no attacking strength. Science comes from growth and ordinary infrastructure; the ability provides no direct Science or Tourism.

Install the collection using the [root instructions](../README.md), then start a new modded single-player game as Karib'il Watar. Bonuses are automatic, with no custom panel. The UU uses the standard Spearman model, and diplomacy uses an original static painting. The portrait is an artistic interpretation, not an authenticated likeness.
