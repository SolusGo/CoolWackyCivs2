# The Republic of First Drafts - strategy guide

[Player handbook](../StrategyGuide.md) · [Matchups](../Matchups.md) · [Full mechanics](../../TheRepublicOfFirstDrafts/README.md)

**Leader:** Editor-in-Chief Marginalia  
**Playstyle:** Institutional variety and permanent first-possession rewards  
**Suggested victories:** Science or Culture

Build or acquire useful new ordinary Building Classes across the empire, then develop diversified Workshop cities. National first recognition and current local building diversity are separate systems and should guide different decisions.

## Opening priorities

Choose affordable institutions that solve real needs while broadening the empire's recognized classes. Do not buy an irrelevant building just because its reward exists; compare its full cost with what the city requires.

## Develop the engine

Use purchases, faith buildings and acquisitions where they make economic sense. Add Revision Workshops to cities with enough qualifying local diversity to support their Science thresholds. Repeat buildings still help those cities even after the empire's first reward is consumed.

## Convert the advantage

Use the permanent Culture/Gold gains and diversified research cities for a conventional victory. Keep military support for Ink Mortars; extra movement and sight do not remove ordinary siege setup or attack restrictions.

## Pros

- Several legitimate possession routes can establish a new ordinary class.
- National recognition remains earned after later building loss.
- Local Workshop Science rewards developed institutional variety.

## Cons and opportunity costs

- Each qualifying class pays only once per game across the empire.
- Wonders and hidden or invalid control buildings do not qualify.
- Workshop Science depends on current local diversity and has a cap.

## Playing against this civ

- Pressure developing cities before their varied infrastructure becomes a durable economy.
- Contest the ordinary research and cultural race rather than expecting repeated class copies to fuel endless rewards.
- Use mobility and range to punish an unsupported siege unit.

## Common mistakes

- Expecting a second city to repeat the same national class reward.
- Counting Wonders or every hidden building toward eligibility.
- Assuming a lost building erases the national reward or that the local Workshop count is permanent.

All custom civs are human-only and multiplayer is unsupported; these comparisons are for theorycraft or explicitly configured scenarios, not ordinary random AI opponents. General counterplay applies to normal single-player games.
