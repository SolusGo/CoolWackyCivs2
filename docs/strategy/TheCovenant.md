# The Covenant strategy

The Covenant is a human-only Scripture memory course within a conventional Faith/Culture game. Prefer Culture or a protected research economy. No custom victory replaces normal Civ V victory conditions.

## Opening priorities

Recall Revelation before moving your starting units. Study its five numbered sections aloud, then hide the text and type the reference and words. The next four turns introduce the other passages. Settle and explore normally between checks; build a Scriptorium early for 2 Faith and 1 Culture.

## Development

Protect population, production and your borders. Tabernacles add 3 Faith and 1 Science while retaining the Temple's cost/technology/maintenance and prerequisite classes. Unaided reviews earn 10 Faith and 5 Culture per era, at most once per turn. These rewards supplement infrastructure.

## Late-game conversion

Use a stable religious and cultural economy for ordinary victory routes. Keep reviewing even after all five passages reach mastery: every tenth campaign turn tests all five, and a mistake resets the passage's clean streak. The maximum review interval is twelve turns.

## Pros

- Frequent retrieval practice links references, exact wording and repeated reviews.
- City Culture and early religious buildings support gradual development.
- Unaided recall converts successful practice into useful yields.

## Cons

- Mandatory recall interrupts play, with no skip button.
- Mistakes or studying forfeit the clean reward and shorten review spacing.
- Stock art is reused; there is no new model, unique military unit or special combat advantage.

## Common mistakes

Do not stop at recognising the verse on screen. Rehearse its reference and the exact words before hiding it. Use the section word count to find the boundary. Do not neglect defense while focusing on Faith. Studying again is useful when stuck, but it restarts the check and clean streak.

## Counterplay

Against normal single-player rivals, keep a conventional defensive army and use diplomacy to protect development. If comparing custom civs in a controlled scenario, pressure its infrastructure and deny safe growth rather than assuming the memory gate changes military strength. These are conditional theorycraft comparisons, not tested win rates; multiplayer is unsupported.

## Named matchup examples

- **The Triumphal Imperium:** avoid presenting an under-defended border. Memory rewards do not counter Victory Momentum.
- **The Republic of Pigeons:** its distributed trade economy rewards safe connections; compare sustained city yields rather than individual recall payouts.
- **The Children of the Kingdom:** both use religious themes, but its prayer choices and Grace are distinct from Covenant word recall. Each retains its own player flow.
- **The Boundless City:** compare compact territorial development with the Covenant's conventional multiple cities; do not assume recall yields replace the Boundless workforce economy.
